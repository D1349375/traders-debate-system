So, what is RSI? RSI stands for relative strength index. It's a momentum oscillator that measures the speed and strength of price movement, not whether price is too high or whether price is too low. The RSI moves between zero and 100 with a default setting at a 14 period. The common reference levels are 70 for strong momentum, 30 for weak momentum, and 50 being the equilibrium. The key thing to remember with the RSI is that it measures momentum, not value. So, one of the biggest misconceptions with the RSI is that overbought does not equal time to sell and oversold does not equal time to buy. In strong trends, the RSI can stay above 70 for weeks. And vice versa, the RSI can stay below 30 during crashes for weeks on end also. So if you just short or long because the RSI is overbought in an uptrend or oversold in a downtrend, you are failing strength which is not trading smart. So the rule of thumb with the RSI is it works best with the trend not against it. So what is an RSI divergence and how do you use them properly? A divergence happens when price makes a higher high and the RSI makes a lower high. And vice versa, if price was to make a lower low and the RSI was to make a higher low, this signals momentum is weakening, not necessarily an automatic reversal. That's the mistake most traders make. They trade divergences randomly in the middle of ranges, which is low probability. So, how do you use divergence correctly? Only use RSI divergences at key levels such as range highs, lows, VWAP highs and resistance or after extended directional moves. In the example on the chart, we have our range high. Price pushes up into it. We get our higher high on the chart and on the RSI. price then makes another push up into our key level which is the range high creating a higher high. Whereas on the RSI chart we put in a lower high causing a divergence which would be additional confluence when looking to short. So a divergence is more confirmation or a warning and not necessarily an entry trigger at any given divergence and requires more context.
